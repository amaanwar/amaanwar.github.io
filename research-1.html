RESEARCH / NIH

Investigating Thrombus Composition with Volume Electron Microscopy

Quantifying platelet and red blood cell organization in experimentally induced thrombi to better understand clot architecture.

Institution: National Institutes of Health, National Institute of Biomedical Imaging and Bioengineering

Research area: Volume electron microscopy, vascular biology, quantitative image analysis

Overview

Blood clots are complex structures formed by interacting platelets, red blood cells, and the vessel wall. This research examines how clot composition and organization vary across experimental conditions in a ferric chloride (FeCl3) vascular injury model. Using three-dimensional imaging and quantitative analysis, I study the spatial distribution of cellular components within the thrombus.

Methods

I analyze volumetric electron microscopy datasets, including focused ion beam–scanning electron microscopy (FIB-SEM) regions, and segment dense and degranulated platelets, red blood cells, and the vessel wall. I use image-analysis workflows to calculate component areas and fractions across sequential slices and compare clot organization at different FeCl3 concentrations.

My analysis includes aligning datasets around regions of occlusion, examining compositional trends along the vessel, and visualizing how cellular fractions change across samples.

Preliminary Observations

Exploratory comparisons suggest that clot composition varies with injury conditions. In the analyzed non-FIB regions, the red blood cell fraction generally increases with FeCl3 concentration, while the fraction of loosely packed platelets decreases. These observations are preliminary and are being interpreted in the context of sample variation and imaging region.

Significance

Mapping clot architecture at high resolution may help clarify how platelets and red blood cells contribute to thrombus formation and occlusion. This work brings together biomedical imaging, segmentation, and quantitative analysis to investigate mechanisms of thrombosis.
